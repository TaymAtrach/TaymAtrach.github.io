About Me

I'm Taym Atrach, a student at McGill University pursuing a double major in Computer Science and Hispanic Studies, along with a supplemental minor in Computer Science. Before McGill, I specialized in Interactive Media Arts at Dawson College. Since I was young, I've always had a strong interest in creating things and understanding how they work. My journey into video game development began in childhood, and that passion continues to grow today. My goal is to create immersive experiences that allow people to step away from reality for a moment and enter new worlds. Through my studies in computer science, I've been developing both the technical and creative skills behind game development, including game engines, user interfaces, level design, gameplay mechanics, and interactive systems. In my creative work, I enjoy combining technical problem-solving with storytelling and design to build experiences that feel original and engaging. Collaboration is also an important part of how I work. I enjoy sharing ideas, receiving feedback, and learning from people who approach problems differently than I do. Working on playable projects under tight deadlines has also made testing a natural part of my development process. I regularly playtest builds, track down bugs, test different interactions and edge cases, and iterate until things behave the way they're supposed to. That experience has helped me develop a stronger attention to detail and an appreciation for the methodical side of software development, especially the process of finding problems, understanding why they happen, and making systems more reliable.

Final Gamble

Final Gamble is an indie psychological horror game set in a seemingly luxurious casino. What begins as a harmless visit quickly turns into a tense and unsettling experience as players uncover the disturbing secrets hidden behind the bright lights and polished atmosphere. By combining environmental storytelling, suspense, and AI-driven encounters, the game creates a strong sense of isolation and uncertainty.

Overview

He'll Hunt

He'll Hunt is a local two-player competitive game created for my first-ever game jam, McGameJam 2026. Set in Hell, players battle to obtain the power to become human and escape to Heaven through a series of portals. The player who holds this power becomes the target of the other, creating a fast-paced experience centered around pursuit, strategy, and constant role reversals.

Overview

He'll Hunt is an asymmetrical two-player game where one player attempts to reach portals and escape, while the other tries to eliminate them and steal the power for themselves. The hunter can become the hunted at any moment, leading to tense and unpredictable matches.

My Role

I worked with a small team during McGameJam 2026, contributing to gameplay programming, game design, and overall polish. Together, we transformed a simple concept into a fully playable prototype within a very limited development window.

What I Learned

As my first game jam, this project taught me how to prototype quickly, prioritize the most important features, and make design decisions under tight deadlines. It also showed me the value of teamwork, communication, and adapting ideas based on what was realistically achievable in a short period of time.

Which Came First?

For years and years, scientists and commonfolk alike have wondered of the origins of a certain bipedal feathered friend, the chicken. Indeed, we still do not know which came first between the bird or the egg... Such questions have produced a fight of cosmic levels... between a WhotsOpp Chicken and a WhotsOpp Egg?! Come on, we've GOT to see how it ends!

Overview

Which Came First? is a local two-player competitive game that settles the age-old question the only way possible: a fight. One player controls the WhatsOpp Egg and the other controls the WhatsOpp Chicken, and the two battle head-to-head to prove which one came first.

My Role

As a programmer on the team, I developed the game manager that controls core game flow and state, implemented controller support for responsive gamepad input, and contributed to the project's visual effects (VFX).

What I Learned

This project strengthened my gameplay programming skills, particularly in structuring a game manager to coordinate core systems and game state. Implementing controller support taught me how to design input handling that feels responsive across different devices, and working on VFX gave me a better understanding of how visual feedback improves game feel and player experience.

Overview

In Bal Arena, you play as a ballerina competing against rival dancers for control of the spotlight. Fight off your opponents to keep the stage to yourself, but be careful: The spotlight will randomly turn on! so you'll need to balance attacking with staying in position.

What I Learned

This project taught me how to design within technical constraints and collaborate closely with programmers so the level supported the gameplay. It also helped me improve my 3D modeling and game design skills.
